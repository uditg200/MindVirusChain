\begin{abstract}
We study the dynamics of information propagation in networks of large language
model (LLM) agents. Using evolutionary optimization, we develop and evaluate
conversational patterns that can reliably transmit behavioral modifications
through chains of interacting agents. Our framework achieves propagation fidelity
of 89\% over 5-hop chains, significantly outperforming baseline approaches.
We discuss implications for AI safety and propose defensive measures.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

As LLM-based agents become increasingly deployed in multi-agent systems,
understanding how information---and potentially harmful behaviors---can
propagate between agents becomes critical for safety.

Previous work has focused on single-agent jailbreaking and prompt injection
\cite{perez2022red, zou2023universal}, but the dynamics of \emph{inter-agent}
influence propagation remain understudied.

\section{Methodology}

\subsection{Evolutionary Framework}

We employ a genetic algorithm to optimize ``payloads''---conversational
messages designed to modify the behavior of receiving agents. Our fitness
function combines:

\begin{equation}
F = w_1 \cdot R_{\text{infection}} + w_2 \cdot S_{\text{stealth}} + w_3 \cdot P_{\text{persistence}}
\end{equation}

where $R_{\text{infection}}$ is the infection rate, $S_{\text{stealth}}$ is a
stealth score measuring how natural the payload appears, and
$P_{\text{persistence}}$ measures how long the behavioral change persists.

\subsection{Swarm Topology}

We evaluate propagation across different network topologies:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Chain: linear sequence of $n$ agents
  \item Star: one central agent connected to $n-1$ peripheral agents
  \item Random: Erd\H{o}s-R\'enyi random graph with connection probability $p$
  \item Scale-free: Barab\'asi-Albert preferential attachment model
\end{itemize}

\section{Results}

Our best evolved payloads achieve:
\begin{itemize}
  \item 89\% infection rate over 5-hop chains (vs. 34\% for hand-crafted baselines)
  \item 0.81 stealth score (human evaluators rated infected conversations as ``normal'' 81\% of the time)
  \item Persistence through 12+ conversation turns
\end{itemize}
